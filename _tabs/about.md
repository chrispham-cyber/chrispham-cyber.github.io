Hi, I'm Chris Pham. I'm a Computer Science student at the University of Central Florida, and I'm mostly interested in blue-team security like detection, incident response and forensics. I'm also on the CCDC and NCAE teams at UCF.

I use this blog as my notebook. When I finish a Hack The Box machine, a Sherlock, or a picoCTF challenge, I write down how I solved it so I can come back to it later. If it helps someone else who is just starting out, even better. There are also a few beginner posts about networking, operating systems and setting up a home lab.

My resume is on the [Docs](/docs/) page.

### What I'm doing right now

- Taking classes at UCF (Security in Computing, Computer Logic & Organization, Discrete Structures, CS I).
- Working a small contract job on Upwork where I review spam reports for an email provider. I check the email headers (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), find where the email came from, and log each case.
- Practicing DFIR on CyberDefenders, HTB Sherlocks and Blue Team Labs Online.

### Things I've built

- [Home SOC Lab](https://github.com/chrispham-cyber/home-soc-lab): Wazuh, Sysmon and Suricata on three VMs. I attack the Windows machine from Kali and check what gets caught. When the default rules missed something, I wrote my own rule.
- [InjectBench](https://github.com/chrispham-cyber/injectbench): a small project where I test prompt injection attacks against local LLMs (through Ollama) to see how often they leak a secret.
- Second Look: a scam checker I'm building for older people. You paste a text or upload a screenshot or PDF and it tells you if it looks like a scam.
- [Log Triage Tool](https://github.com/chrispham-cyber/log-triage-tool) and [Phish Triage](https://github.com/chrispham-cyber/phish-triage): two small Python scripts, one for SSH brute force in auth logs and one for checking phishing emails.
- [KeyLogger](/posts/keylogger/): an older Python project I made to learn how keyloggers work and how they get detected.
